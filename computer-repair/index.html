What we fix

If it’s a computer problem, it’s worth a look. Here’s what comes through the door most often.

Viruses & malware

Pop-ups you can’t close, a browser that isn’t really yours anymore, a machine that turned slow overnight. We clean it out and close the door behind us.

Tune-up & cleanup

The full going-over: updates applied, junk cleared, startup trimmed, everything checked. Often the whole difference between “slow” and “that’s fine again.”

Failed parts

Drives, memory, keyboards, batteries, power supplies. We replace what failed and move your files across — and tell you straight when a machine isn’t worth putting money into.

Files, photos & transfers

Everything moved to a new machine, files pulled off a drive that won’t boot, the photo library rescued off a laptop before it goes in the bin.

New computer setup

New PC or Mac set up and working the way you expect on day one — printer, Wi-Fi, email, and browsers included.

Printers & Wi-Fi

Printers that will not connect, Wi-Fi that dies in the back room, a router nobody has touched since it was installed. We sort it out and leave it documented.

How drop-off works

1. Call and drop it off

3. We do the work

4. Pick it up

We let you know when it’s ready and walk you through what we did, in plain English.

Frequently asked questions

How much does computer repair cost?

A tune-up and cleanup is $45 flat — drop-off, done within a day. Anything past that is $75 an hour, billed in 15-minute increments. You’ll hear the price before we start working.

How long will my computer be gone?

Most tune-ups are done within a day. Drop it off, pick it up the next. Bigger jobs get a real estimate once we’ve looked at the machine.

Do you make house calls?

Sometimes. In-home work is $75 an hour, and there’s a $35 flat trip charge past 15 miles from Beverly. The $45 tune-up is drop-off only — travel is what makes that price work, and we’d rather say so than pretend otherwise.

Can you get my files back?

Often, yes — photos and documents off a drive that won’t boot are the usual rescue. If a job needs a clean-room recovery lab, we’ll tell you instead of charging you to find out.

Do you work on Macs?

Yep. Windows and macOS, desktops and laptops, including the all-in-ones.

Do you do component-level board repair?

Nope. Micro-soldering on a motherboard isn’t work we take on, so we’ll tell you when a machine is past saving and point you to someone who can try — rather than take your money for a guess.

Not sure if it’s worth fixing?

Tell us the make, the model, and what it’s doing. We’ll give you a straight answer — including when the answer is “that one’s done.”
